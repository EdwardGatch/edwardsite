    How it works

    One boss, twelve helpers.

    Kevin doesn't do the work himself. He's the manager. Each helper is a copy of Claude with one small job and its own instructions, like a team on an assembly line. Small jobs are easier to get right, and easier to fix when one goes wrong.

    1 · Find
    ScoutFinds candidate companies
    GatekeeperKeeps only the good fits
    Researcher creditsFinds 1–2 people and emails
    2 · Check
    Verifier creditsChecks each email is real
    AuditorRe-checks a sample from scratch
    RecorderSaves it all in your Sheet
    3 · Write
    PersonalizerFinds one true detail
    CopywriterFills in your email
    ComplianceChecks the email laws
    DrafterPuts it in Gmail drafts
    YouRead it and press Send
    4 · Listen
    Reply analystReads replies, notes what they said
    Follow-up drafterDrafts a polite nudge if nobody replied

    Only two helpers spend money (Hunter credits). Everything before them is free, so bad fits get thrown out before a single credit is spent.

    Set up the project

    One folder holds everything. Here's how to make it, and find it again.

    For this build, a project is simply a folder on your computer. Kevin's rules, his helpers and every day's work are plain text files inside it. (This isn't a "Project" in Claude's Chat tab. Those can't save files or run on a schedule, so Kevin lives in the Code tab.)

    1. Make the folder. Windows: File Explorer → Documents → right-click → New → Folder. Mac: Finder → Documents → File → New Folder. Name it Kevin.
    2. Open it in Claude. In the desktop app, click the </> icon at the top left to switch to Code, then + New at the top of the sidebar. Under the message box, click Project or folder and choose your Kevin folder.
    3. Name the chat. Rename the chat Setup. It shows in the sidebar under Recents. This is chat 1 of your build plan.
    4. Paste the very first prompt below. It builds the folder's skeleton and a map of it.

    Pick the address you'll send from

    And give it an ID card, so your emails don't land in spam.

    Use a separate address for outreach, ideally on your own domain (like you@yourname.com) through Google Workspace. Then make sure three settings called SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass. Together they prove to other inboxes that an email from your domain really came from you.

    Starting with personal Gmail?That's fine for testing (steps 13–15), and Gmail already passes the three checks for you. Before you send real volume, get your own domain (about $12 a year) and Google Workspace (from about $7 a month), so your personal address is never at risk.
    You have this already.Just run the check below to confirm all three settings say PASS.

    6

    Add the safety lock

    Kevin writes drafts. He never sends.

    Your Gmail connector can send email, so you lock that ability away. There are two locks. A block list tells Claude it's not allowed to use the send tools. A hook is a tripwire that stops those tools even if someone changes a setting by accident.

    Paste in chat 1 · SetupLock sending
    Add a safety lock to this project so you can never send email for me. In .claude/settings.json, deny every send, reply, forward and trash tool from the Gmail connector, and every tool that starts or adds people to a Hunter sequence. Also add a PreToolUse hook that blocks the same tools with the message "Drafts only. I press Send myself." Then prove it works: try one blocked call on purpose and show me it was stopped.
    WhyOne wrong email to 100 people can't be unsent. With the lock, the worst Kevin can do is leave a bad draft, which you'll see and delete. Pressing Send is your yes for every single email.

    Teach it your rules.

    This is the part that makes Kevin good. You write down, once, who you want, how to judge them, and what you'll never do.

    The problem

    Ask AI for "leads" and you get a mess.

    Find me 100 leads and email them.
    Done! I found 100 companies and guessed their emails as firstname@company.com. All 100 got the same message.

    Guessed emails bounce, the wrong people get them, and your domain ends up on a spam list by Friday.

    8

    Lead or no lead

    No "maybe" pile. Ever.

    Kevin decides every company the same way. First, hard rules that pass or fail: a real website, the right location, not on your Do Not Contact list, and an email that a tool or a public page gave us, never a guess. Then a fit score from public clues. Above the line it's a lead. Below it, it's out, with a one-line reason. Try it:

    No lead Tap the clues you found on their website.
    Paste in chat 2 · RulesWrite my scoring rules
    Rewrite scoring.md for me in three parts. Part 1: hard rules that pass or fail, never scored (real website on its own domain, location in the USA and Canada, not on Do Not Contact, an email a tool or a public page gave us, never one built from a pattern). Part 2: a company fit score from public clues with points, needing 3 to pass. Part 3: a contact score (right role, where the email came from, how it verified), needing 6. Add five worked examples. Every company and contact ends up lead or no lead, with a one-line reason. No other piles.
    WhyA "check later" list grows forever and nobody checks it. One score with one line keeps things moving at 100 companies a day, and when a call looks wrong you change the points, not your whole process.

    Paste in chat 2 · RulesTurn my email into a template
    Here is my email: paste your email here Turn it into templates/first-email.md. Only these parts change per person: {first_name}, {company}, {where_found} and {personal_detail}. Keep every other word exactly as I wrote it. Add rules: plain text, no links, no signature lines (Gmail adds mine), never pretend we've met, and the personal detail must come from a public page whose link gets saved in my Sheet. Also write a short follow-up for people who don't reply. Then show me three sample emails for made-up companies.
    Selling something?In the US (CAN-SPAM), a sales email must include your mailing address and an easy way to opt out. In Canada (CASL), you need consent; an email address a business publishes, used for a message about their work, can count, as long as their site doesn't say "no unsolicited email". Check the rules where your contacts are. This isn't legal advice.
    Job hunting?Asking about a job isn't selling, so you don't need a mailing address or unsubscribe line. Stay honest: your real name, no fake "Re:", and stop the moment someone asks.
    Research only?An email that sells nothing doesn't need a mailing address or unsubscribe line under US and Canadian law. The day a template pitches a product, a demo or a call to sell, add both.
    WhyAI-written emails all sound the same, and people can tell. Your sentences plus one real detail ("saw your team worked on the school project in Round Rock") read like a person wrote them, because one did.

    Jargon, decoded

    Words you'll hear.

    Claude Code
    The part of Claude that works with files on your computer and runs tasks. Switch to it with the </> icon at the top left of the desktop app.
    Connector
    A key that lets Claude use one of your apps. Also called an MCP server.
    Agent / helper
    A copy of Claude with one narrow job and its own instructions file.
    Hook
    A tripwire that runs before Claude uses a tool, and can block it.
    API
    The door one app uses to talk to another. Your app uses Google's Sheets and Gmail doors.
    Credits
    Your lead finder's money, like Hunter's credits. Finding or checking an email costs a little.
    Verify
    Asking the other side's mail server whether an address exists, without sending anything.
    Accept-all
    A mail server that says yes to every address, so the check can't tell if the person is real.
    Bounce
    An email that couldn't be delivered. Too many hurts your address.
    SPF, DKIM, DMARC
    Three settings that prove emails from your domain really came from you.
    Domain
    Your web address, like yourname.com.
    Artifact
    A private web page Claude makes and hosts for you on claude.ai.
    Scope
    One permission on Google's "Allow" screen, like "read Gmail".
    Client ID
    Your app's public name tag at Google. Safe to share. The secret isn't, and you won't need it.
    Deploy
    Putting your app on the internet at a web address.
    Seed test
    One copy of a real email to your own inbox, to see if it lands in Inbox, Promotions or Spam.
    When something breaks

    Quick fixes.

    Claude says it can't see Gmail, Sheets or Hunter

    On claude.ai, open Settings → Connectors and check each one says Connected, signed in as the right account. Then start a new Code session; connectors load when a session starts.

    The 4 AM run didn't happen

    The computer was asleep or the Claude app was closed. Turn on Keep computer awake in the app's settings and leave the app open. A missed run starts when the computer wakes.

    The drafts have no signature

    Drafts made through the connector may not get Gmail's automatic signature. Ask Kevin to save your signature in templates/signature.md and add it to every draft.

    My emails are landing in Spam

    Stop stepping up. Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC all pass (step 4). Remove links from the email. Drop back to 15 a day and run a seed test each day until it lands in the inbox again.

    "Access blocked" when I sign in to my app

    On a Workspace account, an admin may need to trust the app: admin.google.com → Security → API controls → Manage third-party app access → mark your app Trusted. On regular Gmail, make sure you added yourself as a test user.

    "Google hasn't verified this app"

    That's normal for your own app in testing mode. You built it, so it's safe to click Continue. Never do this for an app someone else sent you.

    "Origin not allowed" or "redirect_uri_mismatch"

    The address in Google Cloud → Clients must match your app's address exactly: https:// at the start, no slash at the end.

    Kevin used more credits than I expected

    Open today's credits.md in the runs folder. Every paid call is listed. If a step wastes credits (for example searching companies that should have been rejected first), tell Claude and it will tighten that rule.
